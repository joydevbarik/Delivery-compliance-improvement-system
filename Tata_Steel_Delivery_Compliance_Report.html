Abstract

In heavy manufacturing industries like steelmaking, delivery compliance is a critical KPI that directly influences customer retention, supply chain overheads, and inventory logistics. Inefficient matching between customer sales orders and yard inventory levels leads to stock discrepancies, delivery delays, and lack of accountability between plant operators and planners.

This project presents the design, architecture, and implementation of the Automated Supply Chain Delivery Compliance Improvement System, a web-based enterprise portal developed specifically for the steel operations division. Engineered on the MERN (MongoDB, Express.js, React, Node.js) stack, the system integrates a live stock validation calculation engine that automatically determines delivery compliance during order placement. The engine checks inventory depth, calculates complied versus delayed quantities, estimates expected delivery dates using dynamic shortage lead-time caps, and triggers immediate email notifications to operators using an integrated Nodemailer SMTP simulation service.

To bridge communication gaps in high-pressure plant environments, the system features a real-time conversational Help Desk ticket management portal that allows operators and planners to collaborate on inventory shortfalls and logistics. Security is enforced through JWT authentication and bcrypt password cryptography, while the frontend offers an outfit-typography driven, responsive user interface supporting a contrast-compliant theme switcher (light/dark mode). The system has been validated through rigorous test suite scenarios, demonstrating high performance, microsecond calculation latency, and high robustness.

Chapter 1: Introduction

1.1 Project Overview & Background

In the current era of industrial automation and supply chain optimization, heavy manufacturing firms face the persistent challenge of aligning production outputs with consumer demands. Steel manufacturing, characterized by high capital expenditures, diverse product catalogs (SKUs), and complex logistical pipelines, requires robust systems to monitor and optimize order fulfillment. Delivery compliance represents the percentage of orders delivered in full and on time, acting as a crucial barometer for supply chain health.

This project introduces the Automated Supply Chain Delivery Compliance Improvement System, an enterprise-grade digital portal tailored for industrial operations. Designed to run as a web-based MERN stack application, it bridges the gap between order entry and physical inventory depth. By validating product availability in real-time, the system mitigates shipment delays, automates delivery scheduling, and fosters structured communication between operations staff and planners.

1.2 Supply Chain Context: Tata Steel Operations

Tata Steel Limited, one of the world's leading steel manufacturing corporations, operates massive integrated steel plants producing hot-rolled (HR) coils, cold-rolled (CR) sheets, galvanized wires, and specialized steel bars. A typical plant consists of multiple yards storing thousands of tons of metal. The supply chain pipeline involves three major phases:

  1. Sales Order Registration: High-volume customer demand intake.
  2. Yard Inventory Allocation: Physical SKU availability checks.
  3. Logistical Dispatch & Auditing: Transport coordination and compliance calculation.

Planners must ensure that every metric ton of steel ordered corresponds to available yard stock. Discrepancies between logged orders and actual inventory lead to missed SLA dates and customer dissatisfaction.

1.3 Problem Statement

Traditional steel supply chain mechanisms suffer from several systemic bottlenecks:

  • Latent Stock Checks: Inventory levels are often updated in batch processing jobs (e.g., end-of-day ERP synchronization). This latency means operators register sales orders based on stale yard stock data, resulting in over-commitments.
  • Manual Compliance Calculations: Planners manually cross-reference order sheets with yard reports to calculate delayed quantities and schedule deliveries. This process is error-prone and slow.
  • Siloed Communication: When shortages occur, operators and planners communicate through fragmented channels (phone calls, spreadsheets, personal emails). There is no centralized log or ticket audit trail.
  • Lack of Instant Confirmation: Operators do not receive immediate, formal records of order compliance calculations, delaying their ability to notify customers.

1.4 Objectives & Goal Realization

The core objectives of this system are:

  1. Real-Time Integration: Synchronize sales order entry with active database yard inventory checks.
  2. Algorithmic Automation: Implement a deterministic compliance calculation engine to calculate complied vs. delayed quantities instantly.
  3. Proactive Email Alerts: Automatically send detailed HTML compliance audit reports to the operator's registered email address upon order creation.
  4. Structured Collaboration: Provide an interactive Help Desk ticket system allowing participants to open threads, exchange replies, and update ticket statuses.
  5. Theme-Aware Accessibility: Deliver a fully responsive, modern UI supporting light/dark themes to ensure visibility in varied plant lighting conditions.

1.5 System Scope & Operational Boundaries

The system is scoped as an Operational Intranet Portal designed for internal plant staff:

  • In-Scope: User login and role-based actions, inventory stock lookups and adjustments, order registration, automatic compliance auditing, conversational ticket logs, and email dispatch.
  • Out-of-Scope: Direct physical yard crane controls, actual courier shipping API integrations, and billing/financial transactions.

1.6 User Persona & Stakeholder Role Definitions

The system identifies two primary roles:

  • Plant Operations Manager (e.g., Aditya Sen - tata_operator): Oversees plant operations, registers incoming sales orders, monitors compliance tables, adjusts inventory levels during emergencies, and manages help desk tickets.
  • Supply Chain Planner (e.g., Rajesh Kumar - operator1): Reviews compliance forecasts, adjusts yard inventories, collaborates on stock shortages, and responds to help desk messages.

Chapter 2: Literature Survey & Feasibility Analysis

2.1 Evolution of Digital Supply Chain Management (SCM)

Supply chain management has transitioned from manual record-keeping to computer-aided MRP (Material Requirements Planning) systems, and subsequently to massive ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) systems. Modern SCM focuses on real-time visibility, automated event management, and predictive scheduling.

2.2 Challenges in Steel SCM and Yard Logistics

Steel manufacturing yards are highly dynamic. Product variations (hot-rolled, cold-rolled, plates, structural bars) require specific storage conditions and tracking mechanisms. The physical size of steel materials means that mismatching inventory leads to massive operational friction, as moving heavy coils to locate stock is expensive and time-consuming.

2.3 Evaluation of Modern SCM Frameworks

Monolithic ERP systems (such as SAP or Oracle SCM) offer high security but are notoriously rigid, slow, and expensive to customize for localized plant operations. Micro-portals developed using lightweight frameworks (like the MERN stack) act as agile overlays, pulling data from core databases to provide operational dashboards for front-line managers.

2.4 Technology Stack Selection Rationale

To achieve high responsiveness and ease of deployment, the MERN stack was chosen:

  • MongoDB: A document-oriented NoSQL database that stores data in flexible JSON-like documents, facilitating easy updates to inventory structures and message schemas.
  • Express.js & Node.js: Express provides a minimalist routing framework on top of Node's non-blocking I/O model, ensuring rapid API responses.
  • React (Vite): A component-based frontend library that enables a responsive, single-page application (SPA) experience with live rendering.
  • Tailwind CSS: Utility-first CSS framework enabling dynamic dark/light theme switching, responsive design, and rapid UI development.

2.5 Feasibility Study

  • Technical Feasibility: The team has extensive knowledge of JavaScript, React, and Node.js. The technologies are open-source and widely documented, making integration highly feasible.
  • Economic Feasibility: No expensive enterprise licenses are required. MongoDB, Node.js, and React are free. Operationally, the system minimizes stock mismatches, reducing delivery delays and saving administrative labor costs.
  • Operational Feasibility: The interface is designed to be intuitive, requiring minimal training. Autofill credentials and easy dashboard widgets ensure high adoption rates among plant personnel.

Chapter 5: Algorithmic Logic & Mathematical Modelling

5.1 Compliance Calculation Mathematical Equations

Let the registered sales order contain:

  • $Q_{demand}$: Requested quantity of steel (demand tonnage).
  • $SKU_{target}$: Steel product SKU identifier code.

Let the yard inventory contain:

  • $Q_{available}$: Metric tons of $SKU_{target}$ physically available in the yard at the time of order placement.

The compliance engine calculates two output quantities:

  1. Complied Quantity ($Q_{complied}$): The portion of the order demand that can be fulfilled immediately from the yard:

$$ Q_{complied} = \min(Q_{demand}, Q_{available}) $$

  1. Delay/Shortage Quantity ($Q_{delay}$): The portion of the order demand that cannot be fulfilled immediately and must wait for production resupply:

$$ Q_{delay} = \max(0, Q_{demand} - Q_{available}) $$

The overall Delivery Compliance Status ($S$) is determined as a binary classification:

$$ S = \begin{cases}

\text{"Fully Complied"}, & \text{if } Q_{available} \ge Q_{demand} \\

\text{"Partially Complied / Delayed"}, & \text{if } Q_{available} < Q_{demand}

\end{cases}

$$

5.2 Lead Time Delivery Cap Calculations

The expected delivery date ($D_{expected}$) is calculated by adding a delivery lead time ($T_{lead}$) measured in days to the order creation timestamp ($T_{creation}$):

$$ D_{expected} = T_{creation} + T_{lead} $$

To optimize inventory turnover and ensure logistical efficiency, lead times are calculated using a dynamic cap:

  • If stock is sufficient to meet demand ($S = \text{"Fully Complied"}$), the shipment is prioritized for immediate transport, resulting in a standard lead time:

$$ T_{lead} = 2 \text{ days} $$

  • If there is a stock shortage ($S = \text{"Partially Complied / Delayed"}$), a longer lead time is allocated to allow for plant resupply, capped at a maximum threshold:

$$ T_{lead} = 5 \text{ days} $$

5.3 Core Pseudocode & Algorithmic Design

Algorithm 1: Delivery Compliance Calculation

INPUT: Order object (orderId, customerId, productCode, demandQuantity)
OUTPUT: DeliveryCompliance object saved to database

BEGIN
    1. Parse and clean inputs:
       orderId <- UPPERCASE(Order.orderId)
       productCode <- UPPERCASE(Order.productCode)
       demandQuantity <- FLOAT(Order.demandQuantity)
       
    2. Query Inventory database:
       inventoryRecord <- FIND inventory WHERE productCode == productCode
       
    3. IF inventoryRecord is NULL THEN
           Create new Inventory record:
               inventoryRecord.productCode <- productCode
               inventoryRecord.productName <- "Product " + productCode
               inventoryRecord.availableQuantity <- 0
       ENDIF
       
    4. Fetch stock count:
       availableQuantity <- inventoryRecord.availableQuantity
       
    5. Initialize calculation variables:
       compliedQty <- 0
       delayQty <- 0
       status <- "Fully Complied"
       daysToAdd <- 2
       
    6. IF availableQuantity >= demandQuantity THEN
           compliedQty <- demandQuantity
           delayQty <- 0
           status <- "Fully Complied"
           daysToAdd <- 2
       ELSE
           compliedQty <- availableQuantity
           delayQty <- demandQuantity - availableQuantity
           status <- "Partially Complied / Delayed"
           daysToAdd <- 5
       ENDIF
       
    7. Calculate delivery date:
       expectedDeliveryDate <- CURRENT_DATE() + daysToAdd
       
    8. Prepare compliance record schema payload:
       complianceData <- {
           orderId, customerId, productCode, demandQuantity,
           availableQuantity, compliedQty, delayQty, status, expectedDeliveryDate
       }
       
    9. Upsert compliance record into database:
       savedRecord <- UPSERT complianceRecord WHERE orderId == orderId WITH complianceData
       
    10. RETURN savedRecord
END

5.4 Complexity Analysis

  • Time Complexity:
  • The algorithm involves basic arithmetic checks and string parsing ($O(1)$ operations).
  • The dominant factor is querying the database for inventory levels and updating the compliance collection. Using indexes on productCode and orderId reduces query time complexity to $O(\log N)$, where $N$ is the number of records.
  • Overall computational time complexity: $O(\log N)$, ensuring rapid response times.
  • Space Complexity:
  • A small, constant number of scalar variables (compliedQty, delayQty, status, etc.) are allocated in memory.
  • The generated JSON payload scales linearly with input size, representing $O(1)$ auxiliary space complexity.

Chapter 6: Technical Implementation Details

6.1 Backend Controller & Router Breakdown

The backend is structured around modular REST controllers:

  • authController.js: Handles login requests, password verification via bcrypt.compare(), and JWT signing using a shared secret key.
  • orderController.js: Validates order payload parameters, registers orders in MongoDB, triggers compliance evaluations, and invokes the email dispatcher.
  • complianceController.js: Orchestrates compliance lookups and re-audits.
  • inventoryController.js: Allows plant personnel to view stock levels and adjust available volumes.
  • helpController.js: Standardizes the chat support pipeline, handling tickets, replies, and status transitions.

6.3 Nodemailer Configuration & Email Simulator Layer

The system uses Nodemailer to send email updates. If the environment variables for SMTP (SMTP_HOST, SMTP_PORT, SMTP_USER, SMTP_PASS) are configured, the system establishes a secure connection to dispatch emails. If these credentials are not set, it switches to local simulation mode:

const hasSmtpConfig = process.env.SMTP_HOST && process.env.SMTP_PORT && process.env.SMTP_USER && process.env.SMTP_PASS;
let transporter;
if (hasSmtpConfig) {
  transporter = nodemailer.createTransport({
    host: process.env.SMTP_HOST,
    port: Number(process.env.SMTP_PORT),
    secure: process.env.SMTP_SECURE === 'true',
    auth: { user: process.env.SMTP_USER, pass: process.env.SMTP_PASS }
  });
} else {
  transporter = nodemailer.createTransport({ jsonTransport: true });
}

This dual setup ensures seamless developer workflows and simplified offline testing, while remaining ready for instant deployment to cloud production servers.

6.4 Help Desk Messaging Thread Processing

Help tickets are saved in the HelpMessage schema. Each reply contains a unique schema object appended to the replies array. This structure maintains message ordering and keeps the entire thread in a single document:

helpMsg.replies.push({
  fromOperator: isOperatorUser ? name : '',
  fromUserId:   userId,
  fromUserName: name,
  senderType:   isOperatorUser ? 'operator' : 'user',
  message:      replyText,
});

7.3 Security Audits & Role-Based Access Control Testing

Endpoints under /api/orders, /api/inventory, and /api/compliance are protected by the auth.js middleware:

const token = req.headers.authorization.split(' ')[1];
const decoded = jwt.verify(token, process.env.JWT_SECRET);
req.user = await User.findById(decoded.id).select('-password');

This process intercepts request handling before controllers execute. In testing, requests missing valid tokens consistently returned 401 Unauthorized. Additionally, the re-audit action in the frontend interface was successfully restricted, rendering only for authorized users (e.g., tata_operator and operator1).

Chapter 9: Conclusion, Limitations & Future Work

9.1 Project Accomplishments

The Automated Supply Chain Delivery Compliance Improvement System successfully addresses the core bottlenecks of steel plant inventory allocations:

  • Instant Stock Checks: Integrates order creation with inventory checks, eliminating stock over-commitments.
  • Automated Compliance Engine: Automates calculations for complied/delayed quantities and schedules delivery dates using dynamic shortage lead times.
  • Proactive Email Alerts: Dispatches detailed HTML audit reports to operator emails upon order placement.
  • Centralized Help Desk: Consolidates issue tracking and collaboration into structured conversational threads.
  • Contrast-Compliant Theme Switcher: Ensures UI visibility in varying plant lighting conditions.

9.2 Limitations of the Current Prototype

  • Decoupled Database: Operates on a standalone database, requiring synchronization logic for plants using legacy SAP/ERP setups.
  • Manual Adjustments: Replenishments are entered manually rather than being automated through weight-bridge sensor readings.
  • Local Simulated Emailing: Defaults to mock logs unless external SMTP credentials are provided, requiring configuration before production launch.

9.3 Future Technical Roadmap

  • Real-time IoT Integrations: Connect yard storage weights directly to the inventory database using yard crane sensors.
  • Predictive Analytics: Integrate machine learning models to forecast SKU shortages based on historic order patterns.
  • Direct ERP Webhooks: Establish secure database webhooks for automatic sync with external ERP databases (e.g., SAP, Oracle).
